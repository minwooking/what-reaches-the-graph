\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this pipeline, how well a graph answered held-out questions followed what reached it. A relation list induced from the corpus, without gold relations, raised answer correctness by 16.3 points on 2WikiMultihopQA and 12.5 on T-REx, and lists induced only outside the evaluation documents also raised grounded QA on both corpora. Once the extractor had a relation list, extraction from every chunk without CQs reached the combined pipeline's grounded QA on the multi-hop corpus, at 106k tokens per unit against 277k for the combined pipeline's first pass and 838k for its full run. Without any list it scored .558 grounded QA against .430 for iText2KG, .445 for EDC and .213 for AutoSchemaKG. On fresh CQs run through SPARQL, the combined pipeline passed 13.5 points more queries than seeded extraction: a simple baseline reaches the combined pipeline's held-out QA, while the CQ pipeline leads on structured queries asked through CQs. At equal fact count, the CQ-guided single pass scored 14.3 and 25.0 points above model-scored and chain-preserving selections of the listed extraction, and above a random subset and the heuristic, CQ-based and chunk-balanced selections. A corpus-induced relation vocabulary with extraction from every chunk is a simple, strong baseline for CQ-driven graph construction, and with a fixed fact count CQ guidance kept more answerable facts than random and question-agnostic subsets.
