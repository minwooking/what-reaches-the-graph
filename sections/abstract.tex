\begin{abstract}
LLM knowledge-graph pipelines generate competency questions (CQs) from text, derive a schema and repair the graph where CQs fail. We remove each step in turn with Qwen on 2WikiMultihopQA (2Wiki), T-REx and HotpotQA, scoring graphs on unseen questions. In our rebuilt CQ pipeline family on 2Wiki, the gain comes from a flat list of 22 relation names, not from the CQs or the repair loop. One extraction pass given only the list (the list-schema pass) leads our full list-plus-CQ pipeline in grounded QA (answers supported by retrieved facts) when the answerer sees up to 60 facts, by 4.6 points with the gold-derived list and 5.3 with a corpus-induced one, and matches it in answer correctness at one eighth of its construction tokens. Deriving the schema from generated CQs instead costs 11.1 points. Seeded extraction, with the list in every chunk's prompt, is 6.7 to 8.3 times cheaper than the repair loop on 2Wiki, which leads mainly on bridge-comparison questions, but stores facts under hundreds of relation names, passing 7.5\% of SPARQL tests against 34.9\% for the list-schema pass. On HotpotQA, whose relations are long-tailed, an induced list lowers plain extraction by 4.0.
\end{abstract}
