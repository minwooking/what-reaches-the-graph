\begin{abstract}
Language models build knowledge graphs from text, but it is unclear which parts of a competency-question pipeline make a graph answer unseen questions. We dissect the pipeline one component at a time on held-out questions, in two corpora and two model families, with competency questions generated from the corpus text. A relation vocabulary induced from the corpus, without gold knowledge, raised single-pass answer correctness by 16.3 points on the multi-hop corpus and 12.5 on the second, and a list induced outside the evaluation documents still raised it. With a relation list from the training split, extraction without competency questions was not distinguishable from the combined pipeline in grounded question answering (difference 0.3 points) on the multi-hop corpus, with fewer tokens and more precise facts. It scored 15.8 to 39.2 points above three public extractors run without a list. At equal fact count and with the relation list, the competency-question-guided pass scored 39.1 points above a random subset of the extraction without competency questions, and it held against further selectors. In these corpora, a relation list with extraction from every chunk reached the combined pipeline's grounded question answering with fewer tokens. We release plans, code and graphs.
\end{abstract}
